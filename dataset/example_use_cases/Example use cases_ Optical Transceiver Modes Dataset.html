1 · Standards compliance audit: pass / flag

Every 400G coherent mode with a stated required OSNR (95 modes from 15 datasheets) is checked against a reference limit for its FEC, and its CD tolerance against a minimum of 2400 ps/nm.

Why it needs this dataset: the check is only possible because OSNR, CD and FEC are stored per mode. A product-level summary could not say which FEC a given OSNR belongs to. Result: 89/95 OSNR checks pass and 80/88 CD checks pass; the rest are flagged for human review, not declared non-compliant.
Reference limits used: CFEC 26.0 dB (as in the paper), oFEC 24.0 dB, CD 2400 ps/nm. The oFEC limit and CD minimum are illustrative placeholders, so replace them with the values from your MSA crosswalk before drawing conclusions.

2 · Power vs performance trade-off

Left: one OIF-400ZR module, whose power steps up from 18.0 W to 23.4 W as the mode’s bit rate rises. Right: W per Gb/s for 251 coherent modes across the corpus.

Why it needs this dataset: energy cost is a property of the mode, not the module label. Per-mode power lets planners model the energy price of a higher-rate or longer-reach configuration, and the right panel shows efficiency improving with rate, which is the kind of benchmarking study the dataset targets.
